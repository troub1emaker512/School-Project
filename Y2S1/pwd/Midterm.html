Comparison of road traffic accidents between USA and Singapore in 2022 and 2023

Project Proposal

1 Aims, objectives and background¶

1.1 Introduction¶

Road traffic accidents are a critical issue worldwide, contributing to significant fatalities, injuries, and economic costs. The nature and frequency of these accidents vary widely across different countries due to factors such as infrastructure, traffic laws, and vehicle density. This project aims to analyse and compare road traffic accidents between the United States and Singapore; two countries with distinct traffic systems, urban layouts, and safety regulations. By drawing insights and consolidating traffic data from both nations, the project seeks to use data analysis to uncover patterns, risk factors, and opportunities for improving road safety globally as a result of my findings.

Research Question:

How do the underlying causes and proportions of fatal traffic accidents, specifically related to speeding and drink driving, differ between the United States and Singapore, and what insights can be drawn to inform effective road safety interventions tailored to varying national contexts?

1.2 Aims and objectives¶

I would like to explore the following in this project:

Different causes leading to fatal traffic accidents

  • Weather & climate (seasonal vs tropical climate)
  • Behavioural factors
  • Geographic distribution (urban vs rural areas)

Using the above key points I would like to explore further:

  • Identify key factors contributing to fatal road accidents in the United States and Singapore, with a focus on speeding and drink driving.
  • Compare the proportions of these factors across the two countries using exploratory data analysis and visualization.
  • Analyze trends in accident causes to help understand the role of enforcement, infrastructure, and cultural factors.
  • Leverage data insights to propose effective, evidence-based road safety interventions tailored to each national context.
  • Assess how weather conditions may influence road accident trends in both countries, particularly the role of precipitation."

For this project proposal, my aims are to:

  1. Decide how much data is required to sufficiently explore the above questions, taking into consideration constraints on time and resource
  2. Decide which publications and articles should be used to allow for a coherent analysis
  3. Collect the data via webscraping and store in a form which allows for data cleaning and analysis
  4. Clean and transform the data such that it is suitable for use in the techniques I would like to utilise later
  5. Carry out some exploratory data analysis to identify that there are trends within the data which make it viable for further exploration

1.3 Data¶

1.3.1 Data requirements¶

To carry out the comparison of road traffic accidents between the United States and Singapore, data requirements were defined based on the objectives of the project. Specifically, the following categories of data were identified as necessary:

  • Frequency and Severity of Traffic Accidents: Data on the number of accidents, categorized by fatal and non-fatal incidents, across both countries.
  • Causes of Accidents: Data highlighting factors such as speeding, distracted driving, intoxication, and violations of traffic laws.
  • Weather Conditions: Information on weather-related accidents (e.g., snow, rain, fog for the US vs. wet/dry weather for Singapore).
  • Geographic Distribution: Urban versus rural accident statistics.
  • Behavioral Factors: Data on human error, adherence to traffic laws, and other driver behaviors.

The datasets used in this study will be selected based on their comprehensiveness, availability, and alignment with the research objectives. For the United States, data from the Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS) and National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) provided detailed accident statistics and weather data, respectively. For Singapore, consolidated reports from the Traffic Police and weather summaries from the Meteorological Service Singapore were utilized. These datasets were chosen to ensure consistency in comparing key metrics, such as speeding and drink-driving fatalities, alongside contextual weather data.

1.3.2 Data sources¶

Primary sources of data will include:

  1. Publicly Available Government Reports:
    • United States: National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), Federal Highway Administration (FHWA).
    • Singapore: Singapore Land Transport Authority (LTA), Traffic Police Department reports.
  2. Data Portals:
    • US: Data.gov, National Motor Vehicle Crash Causation Survey.
    • Singapore: Government datasets on data.gov.sg.
  3. Third-party Sources:
    • WHO Global Status Report on Road Safety.
    • Research articles and datasets from safety institutions.

1.3.3 Methodology for data collection¶

The datasets will be collected using:

  • Web scraping: For publicly available reports and data, tools such as Python's requests and BeautifulSoup will be utilized.
  • APIs: Where available (e.g., Data.gov), APIs will be used for efficient data retrieval.
  • Manual download and cleaning: Datasets available in static formats (e.g., CSV, XLS) will be downloaded and prepared for analysis.

1.3.4 Limitations and constraints of the data¶

Despite its strengths, this study acknowledges several limitations. The US data covers a larger geographical area with diverse conditions, making direct comparisons with Singapore's urban-centric environment challenging. Furthermore, Singapore's data, derived from annual reports, lacks granular detail, such as exact accident locations or real-time weather conditions. These constraints necessitate careful interpretation, ensuring insights are contextualized within each country's unique demographic and environmental factors. Nonetheless, the selected datasets provide a robust foundation for addressing the research question.

1.4 Ethical Considerations¶

1.4.1 Data usage and privacy¶

This study adheres to ethical guidelines by utilizing publicly available and anonymized traffic data. All datasets respect the terms of use and do not include personally identifiable information. Efforts have been made to attribute sources accurately and highlight the limitations of the data to avoid misleading interpretations. By focusing on objective analysis, this project minimizes the risk of perpetuating harmful stereotypes or assumptions.

1.4.2 Copyright and permissions¶

Even though there is no intention to use private datasets, permissions to use data, where required, will be obtained:

  • US datasets from government portals are generally public domain.
  • Singaporean datasets will follow the open-data license stipulated on data.gov.sg.

1.4.3 Limitations in interpretations¶

This project acknowledges the ethical responsibility of avoiding overgeneralizations or misinterpretations of data. The findings will be reported with clarity about their scope and limitations.

1.4.4 Potential impacts¶

The insights derived from this project aim to contribute to global road safety improvement efforts. Care will be taken to avoid presenting data or results in a manner that could be misused for political or commercial purposes.

def extract_full_year_fatalities(doc_path):
    # Load the Word document
    doc = docx.Document(doc_path)
    
    # Extract text from the document
    text = "\n".join([p.text for p in doc.paragraphs if p.text.strip()])

    # Regex to capture year and full year total fatalities
    pattern = r"(202[2-4])\s+\d{1,3},\d{3}\s+\[\s*[+-]?\d+\.?\d*%\s*\]\s+\d{1,3},\d{3}\s+\[\s*[+-]?\d+\.?\d*%\s*\]\s+\d{1,3},\d{3}\s+\[\s*[+-]?\d+\.?\d*%\s*\]\s+\d{1,3},\d{3}\s+\[\s*[+-]?\d+\.?\d*%\s*\]\s+(\d{1,3},\d{3})"
    matches = re.findall(pattern, text)

    # Process matches into a structured format
    data = []
    for year, fatalities in matches:

            "Year": int(year),
            "Month": "Annual",
            "Country": "USA",
            "Fatal Accidents": int(fatalities.replace(",", "")),
            "Non-Fatal Accidents": 0  # Placeholder value
        })

    # Create DataFrame
    fatalities_df = pd.DataFrame(data)

    return fatalities_df
